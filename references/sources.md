# Annotated source register

Research checked on **2026-09-18**. This register records sources consulted through available web text, excerpts, catalog records, and selected sections. It does **not** claim that every book, report, archive, or linked reference was read in full. No historical artwork is bundled.

**Evidence types:** original practitioner/research text; official institutional history or collection record; publisher description; current technical guidance. A museum record can establish an artifact's provenance without validating all interpretive claims in its label. A publisher page can establish date and subject without substantiating detailed claims about an unread book.

The historical atlas summarizes provenance. The principle cards, composition method, implementation advice, and invented examples are the skill author's synthesis. A source adjacent to a modern application indicates intellectual lineage, not that the source prescribed that implementation. Dates below distinguish original work from later editions where relevant.

## Graphic systems, typography, and wayfinding

### S01 — Romek Marber, Penguin grid, 1961 onward

[Penguin Marber Grid — Romek Marber archive](https://romekmarber.com/portfolio/penguin-grid/). Designer archive with retrospective account and recollections. Supports shared layout elements coordinating varied cover illustration; an explicitly older precursor to the main survey period. Modern component-family guidance is an analogy. Do not import geometric proportions as a universal usability rule.

### S02 — Josef Müller-Brockmann, Grid Systems in Graphic Design, 1981

[Grid Systems — Niggli](https://niggli.ch/en/products/rastersysteme-fur-die-visuelle-gestaltung). Publisher record establishes the original date, subject, and applications across formats. Used for the lineage of systematic layout, not as a claim to have read the full book or as empirical proof that grids improve every task.

### S03 — Karl Gerstner, Designing Programmes, 1964

[Designing Programmes — Lars Müller Publishers](https://www.lars-mueller-publishers.com/designing-programmes-0). Publisher description of a facsimile, identifying the 1964 original and subsequent editions. Supports a systematic approach spanning type, image, and method. An older precursor; references to tokens and component variants in this skill are contemporary interpretations.

### S04 — Paul Rand and IBM identity, 1972 onward

[IBM logo history](https://www.ibm.com/history/logo) and [IBM Design Language: 8-Bar](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/8-bar/). Official history and current asset guidance. Supports chronology, continuity, and optical correction of black/white stripe widths. Does not authorize altering IBM assets or establish perceptual equivalence for arbitrary logos.

### S05 — Jock Kinneir and Margaret Calvert, British signage

[Design Museum: Kinneir and Calvert](https://designmuseum.org/designers/jock-kinneir-and-margaret-calvert) and [MoMA: Margaret Calvert collection records](https://www.moma.org/artists/132517-margaret-calvert). Institutional histories and artifacts; the collection dates relevant designs across 1957–1967. Supplies a wayfinding precedent spanning the boundary of the survey. Modern navigation recommendations remain interpretations.

### S06 — Coordinated Olympic identities, 1960s–1970s

[Olympic Posters: Branding — V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/posters-stories-va-collection/olympic-posters-branding-olympics) and [Lance Wyman: Urban Icons exhibition account](https://lancewyman.com/coming-going-lance-wyman-urban-icons-exhibition/). Museum discussion and designer-hosted retrospective. Supports Tokyo/Sapporo, Mexico, and Munich contexts and coordinated visual communication. Credit the relevant teams; these accounts are not cross-cultural comprehension studies.

### S07 — Symbol-sign design and evaluation, 1982

[Belinda Lowenhaupt Collins: The Development and Evaluation of Effective Symbol Signs, NBS Building Science Series 141](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-44543cb0c4a466327ffd2e420fe38f67/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-44543cb0c4a466327ffd2e420fe38f67.pdf). Government research report, issued May 1982. Consulted title/abstract and relevant symbol-design/AIGA discussion, especially printed pages 23–25. Describes AIGA/DOT's 1974 and 1979 stages and distinguishes graphic evaluation from audience effectiveness. Supports caution about supposedly universal icons.

### S08 — National Park Service Unigrid, 1977

[A Brief History of the Unigrid — NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/hfc/a-brief-history-of-the-unigrid.htm). Official institutional account. Supports Vincent Gleason's initiative, Vignelli's contribution, and a coherent system allowing varied arrangements. Especially useful for distinguishing a design system from a single fixed template. No experimental comparison of digital layouts is implied.

### S09 — Jacques Bertin, Semiology of Graphics, 1967

[Semiology of Graphics — Esri Press](https://www.esri.com/en-us/esri-press/browse/semiology-of-graphics-diagrams-networks-maps). Publisher description of the English reissue identifies the 1967 French original and scope of graphic variables and diagrams. Supports cartographic/information-design lineage. Specific dashboard rules here are synthesis, not a paraphrase of an inspected full edition.

## Interaction, information, and expression

### S10 — Xerox Star designers, 1982

[Designing the Star User Interface — archived Byte article](https://guidebookgallery.org/articles/designingthestaruserinterface/). Original practitioner text by David Canfield Smith, Charles Irby, Ralph Kimball, Bill Verplank, and Eric Harslem, hosted as a reprint; Byte 4/1982, pp. 242–282. Supports conceptual models, consistent commands, visible objects, and candid design tradeoffs. Distinguish the 1981 product announcement from the 1982 article.

### S11 — Edward Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information, 1983

[Book description — Edward Tufte](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/). Author/publisher page identifies 1983 original and 2001 second edition; describes graphics, comparison, small multiples, density, and deception. Used for subject and lineage, not a claim of full-book review. The skill deliberately does not turn one optimization such as data-ink ratio into an absolute rule.

### S12 — Ben Shneiderman, Eight Golden Rules

[The Eight Golden Rules — University of Maryland](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/goldenrules.html). Author's text dates the original list to 1985 and labels the displayed version as the 2016 sixth-edition formulation. Supports consistency, feedback, closure, error prevention, reversal, and control. Do not retroactively attribute every current phrase to 1985 or adopt its memory/format examples as universal constraints.

### S13 — Dieter Rams, principles of good design

[Good design — Vitsœ](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design) and [Dieter Rams — Design Museum](https://designmuseum.org/designers/dieter-rams). Practitioner-associated presentation and institutional account. Supports the industrial-design connection between form, use, and intelligibility. These principles are a design position, not a mandate that every interface adopt Braun's visual appearance or minimalism.

### S14 — Don Norman, conventions and signifiers

[Affordance, Conventions and Design, Part 2](https://jnd.org/affordance-conventions-and-design-part-2/) and [Signifiers, not affordances](https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/). Author's essays, with reference to his 1988 book. Supports distinctions between physical possibilities, perception, convention, and cues. Do not treat a publication-platform timestamp as the origin date of every concept discussed.

### S15 — Gerard Unger and Emigre's legibility debate, 1992 and later reflection

[Legible? — Emigre](https://www.emigre.com/Essays/Magazine/Legible). Original essay republished with editorial reflection about its experimental setting. Supports the existence and context of a debate about familiarity, reading, and expression. It does not prove that any unfamiliar face becomes equally readable with exposure or that difficult typography is suitable for operational instructions.

### S16 — Music design across objects and graphics

[Art of Noise — Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/channel/art-of-noise/). Museum exhibition account connects music graphics, listening devices, composition, materials, and cultural experience. Supports a broad cross-media context. It does not document a particular LP-to-cassette artwork conversion, and this skill claims no such verified case study.

### S17 — Ben Shneiderman, The Eyes Have It, 1996

[Original paper — University of Maryland](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/ben/papers/Shneiderman1996eyes.pdf) and [author's contextual account](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/about.html). Primary research and author's history. Consulted the task taxonomy and overview/zoom/filter/detail discussion. Supplies an information-exploration lineage; selecting a detail-first flow can still be appropriate for an urgent known-item task.

### S18 — Ethan Marcotte, Responsive Web Design, 2010

[Original article — A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/responsive-web-design/). Primary practitioner text, May 25, 2010. Supports fluid foundations and changes of arrangement in response to constraints. Directly contradicts portraying responsive design itself as only shrinking. Historical sample code is not a current implementation recommendation.

### S19 — MacKenzie, Sellen, and Buxton, pointing and dragging, 1991

[A Comparison of Input Devices in Elemental Pointing and Dragging Tasks — author-hosted paper](https://www.billbuxton.com/fitts91.html). Primary CHI research. Supports attention to target acquisition, task type, and input-device differences. Fitts's 1954 work is an older precursor discussed in the paper. Do not extrapolate these experiments into universal touch, reading, or decision-time prescriptions.

### S20 — Ritchie and Thompson, UNIX interface composition, 1974/1978

[The UNIX Time-Sharing System — Bell Labs author archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html). Original authors' paper. The available document is the expanded 1978 Bell System Technical Journal version of the 1974 work. Consulted the interface/I/O discussion and provenance, especially sections 6.1–6.2. Supports composability and command interfaces; full-screen TUI layout rules are a separate synthesis.

### S21 — Principles of Universal Design, 1997

[Center for Universal Design — NC State](https://design.ncsu.edu/research/center-for-universal-design/). Originating institution's presentation of version 2.0, April 1, 1997, with working-group credits. Supports broad inclusion, flexible use, perceptibility, effort, and error tolerance. These principles span environments and products; they do not replace technical accessibility standards or user testing.

## Contemporary implementation and verification

### S22 — WCAG 2.2

[Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2 — W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). Normative standard used as this skill's baseline. Consult the relevant success criteria for actual conformance work. The selected checks in this library do not constitute a complete audit, a claim about local law, or native/TUI certification.

### S23 — Reflow

[Understanding SC 1.4.10 — W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html). Informative explanation of a normative criterion. Supports the 320 CSS-pixel width / 256 CSS-pixel height conditions, the 400% zoom example, and scoped exceptions for necessary two-dimensional content. Do not describe a whole dashboard as exempt merely because it contains a table.

### S24 — Keyboard interaction and widget semantics

[Developing a Keyboard Interface — WAI-ARIA APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/practices/keyboard-interface/) and [APG introduction](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/about/introduction/). Official informative guidance. Supports conventional keyboard operation and deliberate focus handling. APG patterns are not themselves normative WCAG criteria, and ARIA does not automatically implement behavior.

### S25 — HTML responsive images and art direction

[HTML Standard: Images — WHATWG](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/images.html). Primary technical specification. Distinguishes source selection for image resource sizes from source selection for art direction. Mechanisms support a composition decision; they do not choose meaningful crops or supply missing image descriptions automatically.

### S26 — CSS container queries

[CSS Containment Level 3 — W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-contain-3/) and [Container queries — MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Containment/Container_queries). Specification and implementation documentation. Supports component-local conditions. Verify feature maturity and target browser support during actual implementation; a specification link does not prove universal runtime support, especially for newer query types.

### S27 — Typography across writing systems

[Approaches to line breaking — W3C Internationalization](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/typography/linebreak.en.html). Official overview explaining differences across scripts, with routes to more specific material. Supports checking real language behavior instead of treating Latin word spacing, wrapping, and line measures as universal. It is an overview, not a complete shaping or localization specification.

## Additional historical perspectives and terminal foundations

### S28 — Punk and post-war expression

[Post-war design: an introduction — V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/post-war-design-an-introduction). Institutional account with collection examples, including 1977 graphics. Supports cultural and expressive context, not a technical UX rule. Use it to resist erasing an explicit expressive brief in the name of one allegedly timeless style.

### S29 — Muriel Cooper and collaborators, Information Landscapes, 1994

[Information Landscapes — MoMA](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/1654). Museum account and work/team attribution. Supports the exploration of dynamic digital information by designers working across print and computation. An experimental work is not evidence that 3D navigation is best for contemporary tasks.

### S30 — NASA graphics programme, 1970s

[NASA Graphics Standards Manual article](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/nasa-graphics-standards-manual/) and [NASA brand guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/). Official account and guidance support an identity applied across media. The article describes a January 1976 manual, while brand guidance dates the worm design to 1975. The article's introductory attribution is not relied on here; verify the original edition for detailed chronology. The skill does not claim a full manual inspection.

### S31 — Engelbart and team, 1968 demonstration

[1968 Demo Interactive — Doug Engelbart Institute](https://dougengelbart.org/content/view/374/) and [archival demo account](https://www.dougengelbart.org/mousesite/1968Demo.html). Institute-hosted archive, annotated descriptions, and footage links. Supports structured navigation and interactive views. This research used the available descriptions, not a claim to have watched all footage or measured its usability.

### S32 — Unicode East Asian Width

[Unicode Standard Annex #11](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr11/). Primary Unicode specification. Explicitly warns that its property is not an off-the-shelf solution for modern terminal emulators. Use appropriate grapheme/display-width handling for the actual framework and environment rather than equating character counts with cells. Consult the version relevant to the implementation.

### S33 — Terminal resize mechanics

[ncurses resizeterm manual](https://invisible-island.net/ncurses/man/resizeterm.3x.html). Maintainer documentation. Supports the distinction between library resize handling and application-specific layout, including `KEY_RESIZE` and pad limitations. It documents ncurses, not every TUI framework; use the actual framework's equivalent.

### S34 — NO_COLOR convention, 2017 onward

[NO_COLOR](https://no-color.org/). Originating informal convention. Supports nonempty-variable semantics and explicit user override considerations. Color suppression is not synonymous with disabling all styling, nor does it prove accessibility.

### S35 — Susan Kare, icon sketches, 1982–1983

[Graphic icon sketch — MoMA collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/188395). Artifact record establishes graph-paper/pixel design work and dates. Supports deliberate small-scale construction. The skill does not adopt the label's broad rhetoric of perfect or universally intuitive icons as demonstrated fact.

### S36 — Adaptive desktop interfaces

[Scaling & Adaptiveness — GNOME HIG](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/adaptive.html). Current official platform guidance. Supports adjusting panes and control placement with window size while preserving functionality. Platform-specific dimensions and recommendations are not universal web or TUI requirements.

### S37 — Pointer target minimum

[Understanding SC 2.5.8 — W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html). Informative explanation of WCAG 2.2's AA criterion, including spacing and other exceptions. Supports the selected web check; 24 CSS pixels is not a claim of ideal touch ergonomics or a character-cell dimension.

### S38 — George A. Miller, memory and information, 1956

[The Magical Number Seven — reproduced with the author's permission](https://www.musanim.com/miller1956/). Original paper presented in a later transcription; an explicitly older precursor. Its discussion concerns information-processing tasks and chunking, not an experiment establishing seven-item navigation menus. Use the actual construct and task, not the memorable title, when reasoning about interface limits.

## Dynamic systems and performance

### S39 — Core Web Vitals

[Web Vitals — web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) and [measurement guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals-measurement-getting-started). Current Google guidance defines the evolving Core Web Vitals set and recommends assessing its thresholds at the 75th percentile. Consulted for LCP, INP, CLS, and the distinction between field and lab measurement. These metrics do not cover every task, energy cost, native application, or terminal interface; verify the current set before encoding it in a long-lived budget.

### S40 — Dynamic status messages

[Understanding Status Messages — W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) and [WAI-ARIA 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/). Normative and informative W3C material distinguishes programmatically determinable status, alert, and log behavior and notes the risk of overly chatty live regions. Used to support careful batching and labeling of streaming status, not to prescribe one token-streaming implementation or claim assistive-technology compatibility without testing.

### S41 — NIST Generative AI Profile, 2024

[Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf). Cross-sectoral risk-management profile. Consulted sections on confabulation, human oversight, source/citation verification, provenance, override, incident response, and recovery. It supports treating these as consequential system concerns; the interface state model and presentation guidance in this skill are synthesis, not NIST requirements or proof that a review screen makes a system safe.

### S42 — Adaptive, wearable, and spatial platform layout

[Layout — Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) and [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures/). Current platform-owner guidance covers context changes, text size, safe areas, watch surfaces, spatial bounds, and alternative input considerations. Used as one concrete platform example. Its point values, materials, and device conventions are not universal requirements; consult the current guidance for the platform actually being implemented.

### S43 — Voice and cross-device responses

[Siri — Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri/). Current platform-owner guidance, revised in 2026, covers responses that can stand audibly and visually, contextual follow-up, errors, and device-independent wording when a request can affect another device. Used as a current voice-interface example, not as general evidence that every conversational system should follow Siri's product model.

### S44 — Performance budgets

[Performance Budgets 101 — web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/performance-budgets-101). Practitioner guidance defines a performance budget as limits on experience and resource measures that inform design and technology decisions. The article's example metrics and byte counts are dated examples, not defaults adopted by this skill. Current projects should choose measures from their task, baseline, supported conditions, and current tooling.

### S45 — Web Sustainability Guidelines

[Web Sustainability Guidelines — W3C Sustainable Web Interest Group](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-sustainability-guidelines/). Current W3C Group Draft Note addresses measurable resource, people, and environmental concerns and explicitly identifies its draft, non-endorsed status and incomplete implementation evidence. Used to support considering resource efficiency and careful claims. Following it does not establish energy, carbon, or sustainability outcomes without scoped measurement.

## Reading beyond this register

For a particular project, extend the research into its actual content and culture: editorial production, local signage, accessibility advocacy, non-Latin typography, professional instrumentation, game interfaces, public-service forms, or other relevant practices. Establish who created the work, for whom, under which constraints, and what evidence exists about its use. Keep examples that change implementation decisions. Do not pad the register with famous books that have not informed the skill.
